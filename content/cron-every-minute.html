Cron: Run a Job Every Minute

Cron every minute: * * * * *. Why 1440 runs a day breaks naive scripts, the flock pattern, and alternatives when 1-minute polling is the wrong tool.

* * * * *

All five fields * — 1,440 runs a day, the maximum crontab granularity. It works, but at this frequency three things will bite you.

The three minute-job killers

KillerSymptomFix
OverlapJob slower than 60s stacks onto itselfflock -n /tmp/job.lock /path/job.sh
Log explosion1440 log lines/dayLog to a file + logrotate
Wrong toolContinuous watching is a daemon jobUse a service/worker, not cron

The flock one-liner (memorize this)

* * * * * flock -n /tmp/myjob.lock /usr/local/bin/myjob.sh

flock -n acquires an exclusive lock or exits immediately — overlapping runs are impossible, and the line still fits in crontab.

When every minute is the wrong answer

If the job is "watch something continuously" (a queue, a socket, a folder) — that is a daemon job, not a schedule. Cron-every-minute is right for light periodic checks, not steady workloads.
